LG Refrigerator Condenser Fan Is Not Working — Possible Fan Motor or Main Control Board Failure

08/29/2026

An LG refrigerator depends on several components working together to maintain the correct temperature. One of the most important is the condenser fan. If the condenser fan is not running, the refrigerator may struggle to remove heat from the refrigeration system, causing longer cooling cycles, rising temperatures, unusual noises, or even complete cooling failure. A non-working condenser fan does not automatically mean the motor itself has failed. The problem can also involve the main control board, wiring, a blocked fan blade, or another electrical issue. Proper diagnosis is necessary before replacing parts.

The condenser fan is located near the compressor and condenser coils, typically underneath or behind the refrigerator. Its job is to move air across the condenser and compressor area, helping release the heat removed from inside the refrigerator. During normal operation, the fan should run when the cooling system requires it. If the fan stops working, heat can accumulate around the condenser and compressor. The compressor may then operate for extended periods while struggling to maintain the desired temperature. This can result in a refrigerator that takes too long to cool, a freezer that becomes warmer than normal, excessive compressor operation, or increased energy consumption.

One of the most common reasons an LG refrigerator condenser fan stops working is a defective fan motor. Over time, the motor can wear out because of continuous operation, heat exposure, dust accumulation, or normal component deterioration. A failing motor may produce humming, clicking, grinding, or intermittent noises before stopping completely. In some cases, the fan blade may move slowly or fail to start even though the refrigerator is calling for cooling. A technician can test the motor and its electrical supply to determine whether the motor is receiving the correct voltage. If the proper power is present but the motor does not operate, replacement of the condenser fan motor may be necessary.

The main control board is another possible source of the problem. It manages many refrigerator functions, including signals that control the compressor and condenser fan. If the board develops an electrical fault, it may fail to send the appropriate command to the condenser fan. This situation can be more difficult to diagnose because the fan motor itself may still be in good condition. Replacing the control board without testing the motor, wiring, and power supply can lead to unnecessary expense. A qualified appliance technician should check the complete circuit before determining that the board is defective.

A failed motor or control board is not the only possibility. The condenser fan can also stop because of a damaged wire, loose electrical connection, or an obstruction preventing the blade from turning. Dust and debris around the condenser area can restrict airflow and contribute to overheating. In some cases, an object may physically interfere with the fan blade. The refrigerator should also be inspected for signs of excessive frost, overheating, or other cooling-system problems. A condenser fan issue may occur alongside another component failure, so checking only one part can result in an incomplete diagnosis.

Several symptoms can indicate a condenser fan problem. The refrigerator may run continuously or for unusually long periods, while the freezer or refrigerator compartment remains warmer than the selected temperature. The compressor area may become excessively hot, and the fan may produce grinding, buzzing, or clicking sounds before stopping. The condenser fan may also fail to spin during a cooling cycle, or the refrigerator may take much longer than usual to cool after groceries are added.

Temperature fluctuations can occur even when the doors are closing properly. These symptoms can overlap with other refrigerator problems, including dirty condenser coils, compressor issues, evaporator fan problems, or sealed-system failures. Professional testing helps identify the actual source. Modern LG refrigerators use electronic controls and specialized components, so replacing a part based only on symptoms can be costly. A technician can inspect the condenser fan, test the motor, check wiring and connectors, evaluate the control signal, and inspect the surrounding components. If the motor has failed, installing a properly matched replacement can restore normal airflow. If the motor works correctly but is not receiving the required signal, the technician can investigate the control board and related wiring. This diagnostic approach reduces the risk of replacing an expensive control board when the actual problem is a relatively simple fan motor or electrical connection.

A refrigerator with a failed condenser fan may continue operating for some time, but the problem should not be ignored. Excessive heat around the compressor and condenser can place additional stress on the cooling system and may contribute to further failures. If your LG refrigerator is running longer than usual, struggling to maintain temperature, or showing signs that the condenser fan is not operating, schedule an appliance inspection. A trained technician can determine whether the problem is the condenser fan motor, main control board, wiring, or another component and recommend the appropriate repair. Prompt service can help restore reliable cooling and prevent a relatively small component problem from developing into a more expensive refrigerator failure.
